Ceremony & Legal

Is the ceremony in your elopement packages legally binding?

No. Our elopement packages include an English-speaking celebrant for a symbolic, non-religious ceremony. Most international couples complete their legal paperwork at their local registry office back home, then celebrate their vows with us in Bali.

Can we get legally married in Bali?

Yes. Under Indonesian law, a legal marriage needs both a religious ceremony and civil registration, and both partners must declare the same officially recognized religion. The process includes:

  • A Certificate of No Impediment from your embassy or consulate
  • A religious ceremony
  • Civil registration by the registrar (Catatan Sipil)
  • Legalization of your documents for use in your home country

Our team guides you through each step. Read more in our Wedding Journal.

What happens if it rains on our elopement day?

It depends on the venue. Resort and hotel venues provide an indoor backup area such as a restaurant or lounge, subject to availability. Outdoor venues such as waterfalls and cliffs have no indoor backup, so the ceremony continues in the same place and umbrellas are provided. Each package page lists what applies.

What is the best time of year to marry in Bali?

The dry season, April to October, is ideal for cliff and beach ceremonies. From November to March, expect passing tropical showers, usually in the late afternoon or evening, while Ubud's jungle valleys are at their most lush.
